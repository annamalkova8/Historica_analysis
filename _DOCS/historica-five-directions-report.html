What to lead with: monthly range, five layers (countries, regions, cities, tribes, events), event scale and Map Builder. What to hold back until proven: "most accurate" and "best design". A claim of accuracy next to a disclaimer of inaccuracy can hurt trust more than silence.

1. Interaction with users

How the product involves users: contributing, returning, sharing, giving feedback.

ProductHow users take part
HistoricaMap Builder (closed beta, researchers only); screenshots; Wikipedia links; blog with guest authors and monthly digest; donations. No game, quiz or open correction found.
PanchroniaNo sign-up. Daily game with five events and a streak, comparable with friends. "What came first" quiz. 91 time "paradoxes" with a share button. Guided tours. Monthly or one-off donations.
ChronasRegistered users edit, rate and add markers and media, link them, ask questions and build article collections ("Epics"). Revision history.
OldMapsOnlineCommunity adds maps. A browser extension detects maps on other sites so users can send them in.
OHMOpen editing in an OpenStreetMap-style editor; daily "on this day" content.
NatagantFree printable posters, tables and colouring sheets; author-led brand.
Where we stand: we are behind. Rivals give people a reason to come back or to contribute. Our contribution route is closed, and we have no habit feature.

Take from others

  • Daily game with a streak (Panchronia). Build it from our events data and keep it free.
  • Shareable cards (Panchronia paradoxes). Make "event A is closer to B than to C" cards with our watermark. Our screenshot tool can carry the same watermark.
  • Suggest-a-correction button and revision history (Chronas, OHM). Keep expert review.
  • Easy submission (OldMapsOnline extension). Let users send a source in one click.

Take from others

  • Pick one main audience for the free map: students and curious adults (Panchronia). This is the biggest group and the one that shares.
  • Keep researchers for Map Builder Pro (Chronas, OHM show the contributor audience exists).
  • Add a publisher and school channel through embeds (Geacron).
  • Write the promise in one line on the home page, for example "Rebuild history with your sources".

3. Quality of maps

Accuracy, data source, time detail, and how honestly the product explains its limits.

ProductTime range and detailData source and honesty
Historica4000 BCE to today; monthly steps in modern eras; layers incl. tribesAI-generated. Own site says maps may be inaccurate. Boundary versions, auto-validation of odd polygons, smoothing. No public sources page found.
Panchronia300,000 years ago to 2012; 1,669 statesScholarly database (Cliopatria / Seshat). States that borders are simplified and approximate, marks disputed areas separately, shows peoples as a paler, rougher background, lists licences.
OldMapsOnline500,000+ scans; boundaries by yearReal documents from libraries and archives; overlay on a modern map.
Geacron3000 BC to today; map at the start of each yearVector database; export to KML and shapefile.
ChronasAbout 4,000 years; 50M+ data pointsCommunity and Wikipedia; edit history. Early reviewers questioned some regions.
OHM4001 BCE to today; uneven by placeEvery feature has an edit history; coverage depends on volunteers.
Where we stand: we lead on range, time detail and scale. We trail on proof. Panchronia explains its sources and limits better than we do, and OldMapsOnline has real documents.

Take from others

  • Honesty labels per area (Panchronia): documented, estimated, disputed. Show them on the map and in the legend.
  • Public sources and licences page (Panchronia).
  • Real scan next to our border (OldMapsOnline). A partnership with one archive would prove accuracy.
  • Data export (Geacron): GeoJSON or KML for teachers and publishers.
  • Edit history on every border (Chronas, OHM). Our boundary versions are a start.

4. Design

Look, onboarding, speed, and how well it works on phones.

ProductWhat I found
HistoricaWeb map with layers and a search panel. No native app found. I could not test the interface.
PanchroniaThree views (globe, flat map, rulers and art styles). Three-step "how it works". Works on phones and can be added to the home screen. Very dark theme colour in its page settings (I have not seen the interface).
OldMapsOnlineTouch-optimised, responsive on desktop, tablet and phone; iOS app.
ChronasHeavy: offers three display modes depending on device and connection; works best on a large screen.
GeacronFree 2D map and paid 3D globe; users have complained about slow loading of the free map.
Paid iOS atlasesSimple slider; animated routes and story pages in one app.
Where we stand: unknown on look and feel; behind on mobile. The speed and phone problems of Chronas and Geacron show what to avoid.

Take from others

  • Several views of the same data (Panchronia): globe, flat map, timeline lanes.
  • Three-step onboarding on first visit (Panchronia).
  • Installable web app (Panchronia) as a fast route to mobile, then native.
  • Touch-first controls (OldMapsOnline) and a light mode for slow connections (Chronas).

Take from others
